# KL_fest.md: Onam Harvest Festival

## Basic Information
- Malayalam Title: ഓണം (Onam Festival)
- English Title: Kerala Onam Harvest & Cultural Festival
- Origin: Kerala, India
- Timing: Malayalam month of Chingam (August–September), lasting 10 days
- Culmination: Thiruvonam Day (The 10th and most auspicious day)

## Short Introduction
Onam is the premier national festival of Kerala, celebrating the legendary homecoming of the mythical, benevolent Asura King Mahabali. Transcending all religious and social boundaries, Onam unites all Malayalis in a 10-day celebration of harvest abundance, boat races, floral carpets, and utopian equality.

## Deep Cultural Philosophy & Symbolism
1. **The Legend of King Mahabali & The Golden Age (Maveli Era):**
   - Under the rule of King Mahabali, Kerala was an ideal, utopian society: there was no poverty, no crime, no caste discrimination, no deceit, and absolute truthfulness.
   - The famous folk song *'Maveli Nadu Vaneedum Kalam'* captures this: 'When Maveli ruled the land, all people were equal and happy.'
   - Lord Vishnu, in his Vamana avatar, sent Mahabali to the netherworld (Pathala) but granted him a boon: to return to Kerala once every year to visit his beloved people. Onam celebrates this joyful annual reunion.
2. **Pookkalam (Intricate Floral Carpets):**
   - Beautiful geometric mandalas crafted solely from fresh flower petals laid in front of homes for 10 days to welcome King Mahabali, symbolizing beauty, growth, and community joy.
3. **Vallam Kali (Snake Boat Races):**
   - Spectacular races of traditional war canoes (Chundan Vallams) powered by over 100 synchronized oarsmen chanting rhythmic boat songs (Vanchipattu) along the Pampa River.
4. **Pulikali (Tiger Dance) & Kathakali:**
   - Dancers painted as vibrant tigers and traditional classical performers recounting epic mythological tales in village squares.

## Visual & Material Features
- **Visuals:** Golden marigold and jasmine flower carpets, pristine white-and-gold Kasavu attires, majestic snake boats cutting through emerald backwaters, and radiant brass oil lamps (Nilavilakku).
